# Decisions

Judgements made without asking, newest last, with the reason for each.

- Name "Graft", tagline "Who works, who arrives, and what the official figures say.".
- Style: the user asked for "more of a gritty style". Applied visually: newsprint paper (#f3f0e8) with a faint grain, Oswald condensed capitals for headings, thick black rules, a black masthead plate, kickers stamped in red. Stamp red #b3261e replaces the magenta accent (5.8:1 on the paper). Light only, still.
- Voice: gritty means terse and direct, not loaded. The neutrality rules in docs/editorial.md still apply in full.
- Folder C:\Users\richy\SourceCode\graft; dev server on port 5180.
- Plan: seven features (jobs, pay, born-abroad, net-migration, visas, asylum, small-boats) and one tool (asylum-support). One over the "about 6" asked for: labour market plus vacancies and pay each fill four or five sections, so merging them would overload a page; small boats and returns share a page instead.
- Titles kept to 2 or 3 words for the gritty voice: Who Works, Pay Packet, Born Abroad, In and Out, Visas Issued, Asylum Claims, Boats and Returns, Support by Area. "Papers Please" rejected for visas as loaded. Name and tagline unchanged.
- All Home Office series are shown as years ending June, built from quarters, so the half-year 2026 is never set against full calendar years.
- Rates and shares are stored as fractions 0 to 1 in every JSON, to suit formatPercent.
- Net migration "highest/lowest" claims only within periods from year ending June 2021 (ONS method break).
- Asylum grant rate uses main applicants (cases), the publisher's definition; claims, awaiting and support use people. "Admin - Deceased" outcomes are dropped from every count, not just hidden.
- Asylum nationality list is ordered by number of claims, with rates printed alongside only where at least 50 decisions; no ordering by rate.
- No foreign national offender tables, NRM outcome tables or any deaths data are read.
- born-abroad owns pipeline/lib/xls.js (BIFF8 reader for EMP06). Series use the latest quarter's same quarter in each year (NSA); 2020 to 2022 marked as extreme caution.
- Support by Area tool: per-area counts of 10 or more are rounded to the nearest 5 and 1 to 9 shown as "fewer than 10", so a banded cell cannot be recovered by subtraction from an exact total. Region and UK totals exact. No ranking of areas, and no per-1,000 rate (no population source among the briefs). Trend starts 30 June 2023 (after the April 2023 boundary changes) and excludes subsistence-only support, which had no area recorded from December 2023 to December 2024.
- Returns compared with arrivals only side by side, never as a share (different dates).
- Pay page's ready selector corrected to `#region-bars .mag-bar` (the class charts.js emits).
- asylum-support pipeline also writes public/data/features/asylum-support.json (a copy of index.json), because startFeature loads features/<page>.json; area files are cleared and rewritten each run so renamed areas don't linger.
- asylum-support: region spellings are normalised to the most common spelling of the same words (so "Yorkshire and The Humber", "East of England"); regionTotal and ukTotal include placeholder rows (`N/A - …`, Unknown) at the latest date, so the UK total matches the publisher's 93,293.
- asylum-support: shareOfUk is rounded to 4 decimal places; the build throws if any quarter from 30 June 2023 to the latest is missing, or if an unknown accommodation type appears since then.
- pay: ASHE annual % change stored as a fraction to 3 dp (5.8 → 0.058); "x" medians/changes → null. No UK two-digit group is suppressed in the 2026 Full-Time sheet, so the fixture marks 33 (Protective service) as "x" to exercise nulls-last ordering.
- pay: ASHE "number of jobs" column is not carried (indicative only). Download mode keeps only the newest ashetable3 zip and rtisa file and re-extracts Tables 3.1a, 3.5a and 3.7a into .cache/ons-pay/ashe3/; cached mode extracts them from the zip if missing.
- pay: fixtures are one `<sheet>.csv` per sheet in a folder named after each workbook; the RTI fixture skips April 2020 to July 2025 (a gap) but keeps February 2020 for the payroll comparison.
- jobs: industry names come from the SIC letter (e.g. Q "Health and social work", I "Accommodation and food", D "Electricity and gas supply"), not the sheet's hyphenated headings. An industry's peak is the first period at its own maximum (ties go to the earliest). VACS02 period labels are tidied before parsing ("Nov- Jan 2002" has a stray space). The rate sheet's `..` becomes null. The vacancies fixture is kept as one CSV per sheet (`levels.csv`, `job openings rate.csv`), and the real modes read the newest vacs02<mon><yyyy>.xlsx.
- small-boats: a nationality absent from the previous year in Data_IER_D01 gets `previous: 0` (the dataset has no suppression, so absence is a real zero); null only when there is no previous year.
- small-boats: a year missing from IER_02a is kept in the heat table as a row of nulls and left out of perBoat; perBoat is rounded to 1 decimal place.
- small-boats: claimed shares are rounded to 4 decimal places; "NRM referral only" is not in the contract, so the three shares need not sum to 1. Ret_06a provisional quarters follow the publisher's own [p] marker (currently 8 quarters); Data_Ret_D01 years use the stated latest-7-quarters rule.
- small-boats: download mode skips a file already in .cache/ under the same edition name; cached mode reads the latest edition by the month-year in the file name.
- asylum: `decisionsPeople` follows the contract (every outcome group except Admin - Deceased, so it includes withdrawals and administrative outcomes: 134,432 for YE June 2026). It is not the publisher's "people receiving initial decisions" (115,872 in Asy_00a = grants + refusals). Do not use it for that headline.
- asylum: `claims.people` is null only for years with a quarter before dependants were first recorded (2002 Q1), so YE June 2002 is null. After that, a quarter with no Dependant rows counts as a real zero.
- asylum: a year counts as provisional if it includes any quarter from the latest quarter's calendar year (2026 Q1 onwards, the publisher's rule). Years ending June are kept only when all four quarters are in the data, so a September edition still ends at the June year. A year in the middle with a missing quarter stays in the list with null values.
- asylum: support `people` includes D09's "N/A - …" placeholder rows (people with no area or nationality recorded); this matches Asy_00a's 93,293. Hotels are null before the first date with any hotel row (31 Dec 2022) and 0 after it.
- asylum: the top ten nationalities leave out "Other and unknown" and keep Refugee and Stateless. Ties are sorted by name. `decisions` is 0, not null, for a nationality with no decisions in the year, and its grantRate is null.
- net-migration: Table 5 `british` is British net migration, as the contract defines it (YE Dec 2024: -17,000 first published, -117,000 now); the contract's example values (-77,000, -257,000) were British emigration. Added `britishEmigration` (positive) to each stage so the "British emigration revised from a to b" sentence has true figures.
- net-migration: revision stages named "First published", "Revised with 6 months of travel data", "Latest" (the last published stage); "Not published" and "Revision for ..." rows dropped. Headers normalised with clean() (notes dropped, line breaks to spaces), not "text before \r\n", which would cut "Humanitarian:\r\nResettlement" and every Table 5 heading.
- net-migration: download mode takes the first .xlsx link on the ONS dataset page and saves it as ltim-<mon><yyyy>.xlsx; read modes use the newest such file, so ltim-nov2025.xlsx is ignored.
- net-migration: the peak on current methods across rolling quarters is YE March 2023 (944,000), not YE June 2023 (924,000) as the brief says; the page computes it from the data.
- visas: a year ending June (or quarter) with no rows at all for one of its quarters is kept in the arrays as null, never a partial sum; occupation topJobs/sponsoredRoutes are emptied (sponsoredTotal null) if the latest year is incomplete.
- visas: Health and Care Worker occupation groups matched by SOC code (613, 223, 221), not by name: the data calls 223 "Nursing Professionals", not "Nursing and Midwifery Professionals" as the plan says.
- visas: "*" in Occ_D02 Grants adds 0 to sums (nothing to add); "*" unit groups (modelled 2021 Q1 to 2024 Q3) are left out of topJobs but count in minor-group and route totals. Industry is not output, so the duplicate "Administrative and Support Activities" label needs no merge.
- visas: provisional = the latest two quarters on file (the publisher's "2026 Q1 onwards" rule, generically); the grant rate for "all" includes the Other group. Vis_01 cross-check of the latest work total warns only.
- born-abroad: levels are output as whole people (the EMP06 cells hold people, though the sheet header says thousands); rates as fractions rounded to 6 decimal places (per cent ÷ 100).
- born-abroad: the share (nonUkBorn / total) is not stored; the page computes it, null when either value is null. "*" and ".." are null everywhere; nothing in this page sums cells.
- born-abroad: caution flags follow the plan by year ("reweight" = 2019, "pandemic" = 2020 to 2022), not by footnote digits, since only the Jan-Mar rows carry note 7.
- born-abroad: ONS links files as /file?uri=…/current/emp06<mon><year>.xls, so the source matches the whole URL instead of findLink (which matches the path only); older emp06 files in .cache/ons-birth are removed on download.
- born-abroad: the fixture is a real .xls built by test/fixtures/ons-birth/make-fixture.js (writer in write-xls.js, test-only), not CSV, so fixture mode runs through lib/xls.js too. Awkward cases altered from real values: "*" EU rate 1997, ".." non-UK level 2020, ".." in duplicate EU column G 2024, no Apr-Jun 2010 row.
- asylum-support page: opens on the first area alphabetically when the URL has no hash (no "biggest" default, to avoid ranking); the area table is shown open as it is the tool's result; a UK share under 0.1% reads "less than 0.1%" rather than "0.0%"; the trend sentence also covers an exact zero at one end ("There were none on 30 June 2023"), since 0 is a real count, not a band; "about the same" means within 5% of the first figure.
- net-migration (page): "peak/most" claims use periods from the year ending June 2021 only, including the reasons and dependants series (keys from 2021-06), for one rule across the page.
- net-migration (page): ONS zeros (under 500) for British flows and dependants read "fewer than 500" in prose.
- asylum page: `decisionsPeople` is not used anywhere on the page (it includes withdrawals and administrative outcomes); decisions are shown only as main-applicant grants, refusals and withdrawals.
- asylum page: the claims peak clause is limited to years with a count of people ("the most in any year ending June since 2003"), since YE June 2002 has main applicants only; the claims chart adds main applicants (s1) so 2002 still shows.
- asylum page: the nationality range sentence gives exact formatted rates ("from 0.6% to 92%") instead of the plan's "under/over", which reads wrongly for sub-1% rates; nationalities are not named beside the rates in prose.
- asylum page: the plan's support sentence is split in two: total on support with its year-on-year change, then hotels with their own year-on-year change, so each "down x%" has a clear subject.
- asylum page: dates in method notes (first year with people, provisional years, first hotel date) are filled from the data, not typed.
- jobs page: line charts plot every third rolling period counting back from the latest (non-overlapping three months, 222 LFS points); table twins list every twelfth (the same three months each year, newest first). Sentences use every monthly period.
- jobs page: LFS rates are shown to one decimal place (local `rate` helper), because formatPercent rounds to whole numbers above 10% and year-on-year changes are tenths of a point. Comparisons ("level with", "above", record high) are made on tenths of a point.
- jobs page: the x axis is numeric decimal years with `formatX`, because the shared time axis puts a year every four years at phone width, which runs together over 1971 to 2026.
- jobs page: "record high" / "the most since the series began" clauses need more than twelve periods of history; the vacancies "three months before" is the period three back (non-overlapping). Industry percentage changes (table only) follow the plan guard (latest at least 10% of own peak and at least 5,000), otherwise "levels only".
- pay: region bars carry a red marker at the UK full-time median (markedBars) so each region reads against the UK figure; the method note says regional medians do not average to it.
- pay: ASHE "provisional" sentence in the method note is generated from `ashe.provisional`, not typed.
- pay: RTI industry T-U ("Households and Extraterritorial" in the JSON) is shown as "Households as employers and extraterritorial organisations" (the SIC section names) on the page only.
- pay: RTI home-region medians (in the contract, not in a planned section) are shown as a table under the payroll section, since its method note mentions region by home.
- pay: payroll comparisons are stated as differences ("1.2 million more than in February 2020") rather than the February 2020 value, so two 2-s.f. millions never read as equal; the year-earlier month is matched by key, not by position, so gaps in the series don't mislead.
- pay: caring rank phrase only for 8+ published groups (quarters: highest-paid quarter / top half / bottom half / lowest-paid quarter) and skipped when caring is itself the top or bottom group.
- pay: occupation and industry bars put the name on its own line under 40em so 26 long SOC names stay readable at 360px.
- small-boats page: hero adds a tile for small boats' share of the four detection methods (`detections`), "of detected irregular arrivals", base = sum of the four; shown only if the base is at least 100.
- small-boats page: nationality sentence reads "Most small boat arrivals … (65%) were recorded with one of five nationalities: …" when the top five exceed half, otherwise "{pct} of small boat arrivals … were recorded with one of five nationalities"; no "most common" or other ranking words. Base is all arrivals in the year, including nationality not recorded.
- small-boats page: "busiest month on record" is written "the busiest month since {first year in IER_02a}", since the series starts in 2018. The heat table's Total column is left unshaded so the totals don't flatten the month shades.
- small-boats page: claimed shares use the latest arrival year with at least 100 arrivals; "claimed asylum" = asylumOnly + asylumAndNrm (both of arrivals). If asylumAndNrm is null the sentence gives asylumOnly alone, labelled "without a modern slavery referral".
- small-boats page: the month section uses full width on desktop (src/styles/small-boats.scss) so the 14-column heat table needs no sideways scroll at 1024px and up.
- born-abroad page: the "One in five" h2 is generated from the latest share (nearest one-in-n within 3 points, else the HTML fallback "Born outside the UK"), so the heading stays true; sentences use percentages unless oneIn fits both years.
- born-abroad page: the pandemic caution years are drawn as a second, dashed series ("Extreme caution years") joined to the years either side, since line-chart.js has no per-segment styling; the caution and reweighting years in the method note are filled from the data's caution flags.
- born-abroad page: chart labels use the ONS short terms (UK born, Non-UK born, EU born, Non-EU born) to fit the direct-label margin; the method note says non-EU born means born outside both the UK and the EU, and sentences say it in full.
- born-abroad page: EU/non-EU comparison drops the base-year figure in brackets when it rounds to the same as the latest (2.3 million both), and says "about the same" within 0.5%; rates are shown to one decimal so a gap over 0.5 points never prints as equal.
- net-migration (page): the nationality chart shows net migration by nationality (British, EU+, non-EU+) from YE June 2021, now that createChart draws below zero; the latest-year bars show size by length and direction by the printed sign.
- net-migration (page): the June 2021 method break is a "New method" marker on the net, reasons and dependants charts (reasons periods have no newMethod flag, so key >= 2021-06), plus the method notes and a note under the net chart.
- visas page: the groups summary adds a grant-rate clause (all groups, highest and lowest of work/study/family/visitor; "other" left out as a mixed bag), since the plan's method note defines grant rates but no sentence used them.
- visas page: the care method note gives the series start from the data ("starts in October to December 2020") rather than a route opening date, since the Health and Care visa existed under Tier 2 before the points-based routes began; policy dates (11 March 2024, 22 July 2025) are stated as dated facts.
- visas page: Health and Care Worker occupation groups are shown as a heat table (four groups by year ending June) as the twin for the occupation sentence; "Doctors" = SOC 221, "Nurses" = SOC 223 (named "Nursing professionals" in the table, as in the data).
- visas page: dependants shown as two bar lists (one per route) on a common scale, not as a ratio; the sentence gives shares of each route's visas, never dependants per worker. Counts only below 100 visas on a route.
- visas page: "fewest/most since" names the last earlier year at or beyond the latest figure and is omitted when that is the year before (no record to report).
- visas page: every method note says which source it uses (visa data, all applicant types; or occupation data, main applicants only); the provisional sentence is built from the first provisional quarter.
